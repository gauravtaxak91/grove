Privacy policy

Grove from the App Store (“Grove: Learn English”), version 4.0, for iPhone, iPad and Mac, and its share extension; and Grove 4.0 for Android phones and tablets, Windows PCs and Macs, downloaded from this website.
Effective date: 11 November 2026.

This page says what Grove keeps, where it keeps it, when anything leaves your device, and how to delete it. It is written for learners, parents and teachers, and it describes what the app actually does.

In short

  • Grove does not collect personal data. Nothing you read, write, say or score is sent to the developer.
  • There is no account, no advertising, no analytics and no tracking, and no code from any advertising, analytics or tracking company.
  • Your work is kept on your own device, and you can delete it in Settings.
  • Grove from the App Store (iPhone, iPad and Mac) goes online only if you switch on iCloud sync. It never sends your voice anywhere: speech is turned into words on the device, or not at all. Share ▸ Grove takes the article from the page Safari already has open, and Grove itself fetches nothing.
  • Grove from this website (Android, Windows and Mac) goes online only when you press Check for updates (it then reads one small file on this website), when you share a web link to it on Android (it then opens that one page to pick the article out), or, on an Android device that cannot recognise speech by itself, after someone has said yes to the device’s online speech service. It has no sync. See Grove from this website.
  • Grove is the same for every learner and does not ask who is learning. The dictionary hides rude and offensive words until someone turns that off.

1. Who makes Grove

Grove is made by Gaurav Taxak (“the developer”, “we”). You can write to gauravtaxak@gmail.com about anything on this page.

2. What Grove does not collect

Grove has no server of its own, and nothing in the app sends information to the developer. In particular, Grove does not:

  • ask for a name, an email address, an age or a birth date, or have any account or sign-in;
  • send what you read, write, say, record or score to the developer or to anyone else;
  • show advertising, or contain analytics, tracking or crash-reporting code of its own;
  • use your location, contacts, photos, camera or advertising identifier;
  • include code from other companies that collects or sends anything. Every version of Grove is built with Flutter (Google’s open-source toolkit for apps) and a few open-source packages, among them PDFium (the PDF engine of Google Chrome) and SQLite, each read through to make sure it collects nothing and sends nothing. On iPhone, iPad and Mac, the parts that work with the system (the microphone, speech recognition, iCloud, files and the share extension) are Grove’s own code, written with Apple’s own frameworks.

The privacy manifests of the App Store app and its share extension, which Apple reads, declare no tracking and no collected data.

About Apple. Apple runs the App Store and iCloud. If you have agreed in your device’s settings to share analytics with app developers, Apple may pass crash reports and general app statistics to the developer. That is Apple’s service, described in Apple’s privacy policy; Grove adds nothing to it.

4. The microphone and speech recognition

When Grove asks

Grove asks your device for the microphone the first time a learner chooses to speak: to read a passage aloud, to say a word, or to answer a speaking task. On iPhone, iPad and Mac, Grove from the App Store also asks for speech recognition. You can say no, or turn either off later in your device’s privacy settings; every speaking task can then be done aloud without recording.

What is recorded and kept

  • Reading aloud and saying a word: Grove listens to show which words came through clearly. Nothing is saved to a file or kept.
  • Speaking tasks: the answer is recorded to a temporary file so the learner can play it back. It is kept only if the learner keeps the answer. A take that is tried again or not kept is deleted at once, and any left over are deleted the next time Grove opens. Kept recordings stay on the device, outside device backups, until you delete them.
  • Speech recognition only shows what it heard. Nothing a learner says is scored by it.

Where speech is turned into words

  • iPhone, iPad and Mac (Grove from the App Store): only on the device. Grove uses the device’s own speech recognition and requires it to work on the device, so the voice never leaves it. Grove never uses Apple’s online speech service, and never asks to. A device that cannot recognise English by itself (for example, when Dictation is turned off) records spoken answers and plays them back, and nothing more.
  • Android: on the device wherever the device can; on a device that cannot, only after someone has said yes to the device’s online speech service. See section 8.
  • Windows, and Macs with Grove from this website: Grove does not turn speech into words. It records spoken answers so that the learner can play them back.

5. iCloud sync (optional)

Only in Grove from the App Store, on iPhone, iPad and Mac.

  • Off until you turn it on: Settings ▸ Across your devices ▸ Keep my devices in step. It works only when the device is signed in to iCloud.
  • Your own iCloud account: Grove uses Apple’s iCloud key-value storage to carry a small amount of data between your devices signed in to the same Apple account. Grove has no server, and nothing in Grove sends this data to the developer.
  • What travels: where you stopped in each book (the chapter, stored under the book’s title), the words you kept in Words I Met (with their sentences), your highlights and notes (with the passages they mark), and your progress in the course (review cards, checks, the placement result and timed-test results).
  • What never travels: drafts and written answers, recordings, the answers given in each timed-test sitting, reading speeds, look-up history, books, PDFs and saved web pages, drawings, notebooks, and settings.
  • Turning it off stops Grove sending or taking in anything more. It does not empty what is already in iCloud. To take everything Grove has put in iCloud out of it, use Settings ▸ Across your devices ▸ Remove Grove’s copy from iCloud…; it also turns sync off on that device. Turn sync off on your other devices first, or they will put their own copy back. Delete this learner’s work clears the course progress kept in iCloud, whether sync is on or not. A word you remove from Words I Met while sync is on is removed on your other devices too.
  • iCloud is Apple’s service; Apple’s privacy policy covers it.

6. Web pages shared to Grove

  • From Safari, on iPhone, iPad and Mac: choosing Share ▸ Grove picks the article (its headline, site name, author line, text and web address) out of the page that is already open in Safari. The share extension leaves it in a folder on your device that only Grove and its extension share. The extension sends nothing over the internet, and Grove fetches nothing: if Safari cannot hand over the article, nothing is saved, and Grove says how to bring the page in another way (save it as a PDF, then open that).
  • The article is saved as a page in Grove’s own folder on the device, with the site it came from named on it. Remove from the library, in Read, deletes Grove’s saved copy of the page.
  • For Android, see section 8.
  • The website’s own privacy policy covers what the website does when you visit it.

7. Books, notebooks and PDFs

  • Grove opens EPUB, PDF, Word (.docx), RTF, Markdown, HTML and plain-text files that you choose, and reads or converts them on the device, with no internet connection. Nothing in them is sent anywhere, and a picture, font or script inside a book cannot reach the web.
  • On iPhone, iPad and Mac, Grove from the App Store copies a book you open into its own folder first, because Apple lets an app read a file you chose from elsewhere only for a while. That copy is left out of device backups. (For Android, Windows and Grove for Mac from this website, see section 8.)
  • Read keeps a library of the books you open, with folders, colours and pins of your own, until you take a book off with Remove from the library.
  • The pen. Drawings, handwriting, highlighter strokes and tapes laid on a book’s or a PDF’s pages are kept in Grove’s own folder on the device, one file for each book, beside its highlights and notes. They are never sent anywhere, and the book itself is not changed. They are deleted with the pen’s own tools (Clear this page, Clear this book’s drawings), or with the app.
  • Notebooks you make in Read (their titles, covers and pages, and what is written on them) are kept in Grove’s own folder on the device. A notebook’s passcode is not kept: only a check made from it, which cannot be turned back into the passcode. A locked notebook is hidden in Grove, not encrypted.
  • PDFs that Grove makes, by arranging a PDF’s pages, adding blank pages for notes, or merging PDFs into one, are kept in Grove’s own folder, and the PDFs they were made from are not changed. Save a copy puts a copy where you choose. Removing a notebook from the library deletes it with all its pages; removing a PDF Grove made deletes that file.
  • A web or email link that you tap in a book or PDF opens in your browser or mail app, outside Grove. Grove itself does not fetch it, and the website’s or mail app’s own privacy policy covers what happens there.
  • In Settings, Privacy policy on the web opens this page in your browser.

8. Grove from this website

Grove for Android, Windows and Mac, downloaded from this website, is made from the same code, course, dictionary and books, and keeps the same promise: no account, no advertising, no analytics, no tracking, and nothing sent to the developer. Everything above applies to it, except where this section says otherwise.

What is different

  • No iCloud, and no sync of any kind. Section 5 does not apply. Each device keeps its own copy, and nothing travels between devices.
  • Web pages shared to Grove (Android). Text you select in a browser and share to Grove is read exactly as it was handed over and saved as an article in Grove’s own folder; nothing is fetched. A browser on Android passes only the link when you share a page, so Grove opens that one page once, out of sight, to pick the article out, and only secure (https) pages. As with any visit, the website receives the request (for example, your device’s internet address) and may run its own scripts while the page loads; Grove fetches no pictures for it and keeps no cookies or other website data from that visit. The article is saved in Grove’s own folder, with the site it came from named on it, and Remove from the library in Read deletes it.
  • Books and files. On Android, a book you open or share is copied into Grove’s own folder first, because Android lets an app read a chosen file only for a short while; that copy is left out of device backups. On Windows and the Mac, a book you open is read where it is.

Check for updates

Grove from this website is not in an app store, so nothing updates it by itself. Settings ▸ This version ▸ Check for updates reads one small file on this website (version.json), only when you press it, and says whether a newer version is ready, with a link to this website. As with any visit, the website’s host (GitHub Pages) receives the request, including your device’s internet address; nothing about you or your work is sent. Grove does not check by itself.

Speech recognition

  • Android: where the device can turn English speech into words by itself (Android 12 and later, with an on-device recogniser), Grove makes it do so on the device, and the voice does not leave the device.
  • On an Android device that cannot, recognition would mean sending the sound to the device’s online speech service (Google’s, or the device maker’s). Grove asks once whether it may, and the question says that where a child is learning, a grown-up should answer. Nothing is sent until someone says yes, there or with the switch in Settings ▸ Speech and recordings; until then, Grove only records. What that service does with speech is covered by its own privacy policy, not by this one; the developer receives none of it.
  • Windows and Mac: Grove does not turn speech into words. It records spoken answers so that the learner can play them back, and that is all.
  • Recordings are kept as section 4 says: only when the learner keeps the answer, on the device, outside device backups.

Permissions on Android

  • Internet, used only for Check for updates and to open the page of a link shared to Grove. (The online speech service, where someone has allowed it, is the device’s own and reaches the internet by itself.)
  • Microphone, asked for the first time a learner records a spoken answer or reads aloud to the device. You can say no, or turn it off later in the device’s settings; every speaking task can then be done aloud without recording.

On Windows, whether apps may use the microphone is set in Settings ▸ Privacy & security ▸ Microphone. On a Mac, the Mac asks the first time Grove records whether it may use the microphone; that is changed in System Settings ▸ Privacy & Security ▸ Microphone.

Where it is kept

  • Android: in Grove’s own folder on the device, which other apps cannot read. Android’s own backup (to the device owner’s Google account, when it is turned on) may include Grove’s progress, work, settings and articles, as it does for any app; recordings, the copies of books, the PDFs Grove makes and the dictionary Grove unpacks are left out of it (Android stops backing up an app whose data passes 25 MB). To keep a PDF Grove made safe, use Save a copy.
  • Windows: in the Windows user’s own app-data folder (AppData\Roaming\Gaurav Taxak\Grove). A spoken answer that is recorded but not kept waits in the user’s temporary folder until it is deleted.
  • Mac: in the Mac user’s own Library folder: ~/Library/Application Support/com.gauravtaxak.grove.desktop, with its settings in ~/Library/Preferences and passing files in ~/Library/Caches, each under that name. A spoken answer that is recorded but not kept waits in that user’s temporary folder until it is deleted. Time Machine, when it is turned on, backs Grove’s folder up with the rest of that user’s files, except the recordings and the dictionary Grove unpacks, which Grove leaves out of it.

How to delete it

The steps in How to delete your data are the same in this version. To remove everything: on Android, uninstall Grove (this deletes its folder); on Windows, uninstall it in Settings ▸ Apps, which removes what it kept for the Windows user who uninstalls it; on a Mac, move Grove to the Bin, then delete the folders named com.gauravtaxak.grove.desktop in your Library (in Finder, Go ▸ Go to Folder, ~/Library): in Application Support, in Caches and in Preferences. An older Android backup, or a Time Machine backup, may still hold Grove’s data until that backup is replaced.

9. Sharing your work

Two places let you copy your work as text: Copy on a writing or speaking task, and Copy as Markdown on a book’s highlights and notes. You paste it where you choose, for example in a message to a teacher. Grove never sends your work by itself, and it has no way to send a recording anywhere.

10. How to delete your data

Open Settings with the gear button.

  • Settings ▸ Privacy ▸ Delete this learner’s work… deletes from this device: progress in the course and its checks, review cards, timed-test results and sittings, drafts and plans, kept answers, recordings, reading speeds and the look-up history. It also resets the learner’s own settings (the way through the first steps, and Take it slower), and in Grove from the App Store it clears the copy of the course progress in iCloud. The device’s settings stay, among them the voice, the text size and whether the dictionary hides rude and offensive words. It asks first, and it cannot be undone. On a learner’s other devices, do the same there.
  • Settings ▸ Speech and recordings ▸ Delete all recordings… deletes only the recordings. The answers stay, without their sound.
  • Books, notebooks, PDFs, words, notes and drawings are not removed by the steps above. Take a book, a notebook (with all its pages) or a PDF Grove made (with its file) off the library in Read with Remove from the library, a word with Remove from Words I Met, highlights and notes in Notes & Highlights, and drawings with the pen’s Clear this page or Clear this book’s drawings.
  • Everything: delete the app. On iPhone, iPad and Android this deletes everything Grove keeps on the device; on Windows, uninstalling Grove removes what it kept for that Windows user. A Mac may keep an app’s folder after the app is gone: with Grove from the App Store, use Delete this learner’s work and the steps above before you delete it; for Grove from this website, see section 8.
  • Backups and iCloud: an older device backup may still hold Grove’s data (never recordings) until that backup is replaced. For what stays in iCloud, see iCloud sync.

Because none of this data reaches the developer, we have no copy to delete on our side.

11. Children

Grove is made for school pupils as well as adults. It is the same for every learner: it does not ask who is learning, or anyone’s age, and every stage, its practice questions and its timed tests are open to all. Choose with a child what to use, and sit with a young child.

  • No personal data is collected from children. Grove collects no personal data from anyone, so a child’s name, voice, writing, results and reading never reach the developer.
  • A child’s voice. On iPhone, iPad and Mac, Grove from the App Store turns speech into words only on the device and never sends a voice anywhere. On an Android device that cannot recognise speech by itself, Grove asks once whether it may send speech to the device’s online speech service, and sends nothing until someone says yes; the question says that where a child is learning, a grown-up should answer, and the answer can be changed in Settings ▸ Speech and recordings. On Windows and with Grove for Mac from this website, Grove only records.
  • The dictionary’s filter. Settings ▸ Look up ▸ Hide rude and offensive words is on until someone turns it off. While it is on, the dictionary hides offensive words and slurs, and explicit sexual words and senses, in Look up, in search by meaning and on the word card, while words a school teaches for the body, health and keeping safe stay. It is a setting of the device, so Delete this learner’s work leaves it as it is. No word list is perfect, so a grown-up should still be near a young child who looks words up.
  • Links. A web or email link in an imported book or PDF opens in the device’s browser or mail app, outside Grove (see Books, notebooks and PDFs).
  • iCloud, if a grown-up turns it on (Grove from the App Store only). The place in each book, course progress, kept words, highlights and notes then travel through the device’s own iCloud account, which is Apple’s service. The developer still receives nothing.
  • No contact with others. Grove has no chat, no messages, no profiles and no way for a child to reach other people through it.
  • Shared devices. Grove has no pupil accounts, so on a class set of devices or a family device one learner’s drafts and answers can be seen by the next. Use Delete this learner’s work before a device is passed on.

12. How this fits the law

This section explains, in plain words, how Grove’s design relates to the main laws on children’s data. It is not legal advice.

India: Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023

The Act sets duties for anyone who processes people’s digital personal data. For children (under 18) it adds more, including verifiable consent from a parent and a ban on tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted advertising aimed at children. Grove does not collect or receive any child’s personal data, and there is no tracking, behavioural monitoring or advertising of any kind by the developer. The progress Grove records is kept on the device for the learner’s own use, under the control of the family or school that uses it.

United States: Children’s Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA)

COPPA applies to apps and websites that collect personal information from children under 13. Grove does not collect personal information from children: nothing a child types, says or records is sent to the developer. On iPhone, iPad and Mac, a child’s voice never leaves the device. On Android, speech goes to the device’s online speech service only on a device that cannot recognise speech by itself, and only after a yes that, where a child is learning, a grown-up should give.

United Kingdom and European Union: GDPR

Because nothing from Grove reaches the developer, the developer holds no personal data about you or your child from the app, so there is nothing for us to give access to, correct or erase. The data Grove keeps is on your device, under your control: you can see it in the app and delete it as described above. Grove starts with the private choices: sync is off, speech is never sent anywhere without a yes (and on Apple devices never at all), and the dictionary hides rude and offensive words. If you email us, we hold that email (see below), and you can ask us to delete it.
